# Variants — One-Star Review of Something Essential

Judge criteria, in order: (1) one-star review of an everyday essential, deadpan,
about the thing itself; (2) sharpest TRUE grievance, small daily annoyance
everyone recognises; (3) deadpan held, no winking, no exclamation marks;
(4) turn in the last few words; (5) tight and specific, one joke only.
All variants are single-line, ASCII, no exclamation marks, ≤160 chars.
"One star." is placed FIRST in every variant: it frames the review immediately
(criterion 1) and leaves the sting as literally the last words (criterion 4).

## The 8 variants

| # | Subject | Text | Chars |
|---|---------|------|-------|
| V1 | sleep | One star. Sleep takes a third of my life and has never once let me wake up feeling rested. | 90 |
| V2 | sun | One star. The sun shows up on time every morning and has never once learned to knock first. | 91 |
| V3 | gravity | One star. Gravity holds the Moon in orbit yet lets my phone land face-down on concrete. | 87 |
| V4 | Monday | One star. Monday arrives on time every week, brings nothing, and stays all day. | 79 |
| V5 | Wi-Fi | One star. Wi-Fi works flawlessly until the one call that matters, then remembers it has somewhere else to be. | 109 |
| V6 | water | One star. Water has no taste, no colour, and still expects me to drink eight glasses of it a day. | 97 |
| V7 | alarm | One star. The alarm went off at 6am sharp, exactly as requested, which is precisely the problem. | 96 |
| V8 | snooze | One star. The snooze button offers nine extra minutes and charges a full morning for them. | 90 |

## Pairwise tournament (winner takes the round, judged on the criteria above)

- V3 vs V1: both canonical essentials. V1's grievance (waking up tired) is
  universal, but V3 is more specific and visual (criterion 5) and its
  scale contrast (Moon vs phone) makes the sharper turn. **V3 wins by a hair.**
- V3 vs V2: "knock first" is a metaphor joke, slightly wink-ish; the morning-glare
  grievance is less sharp than the phone face-down. **V3 wins.**
- V3 vs V4: Monday is the most obvious subject in the brief's examples — the
  most crowded angle; V3 is more original and more specific. **V3 wins.**
- V3 vs V5: Wi-Fi is a service, a weaker "essential" than gravity (gravity is
  named in the brief itself); V5's turn is good but V3 wins on criterion 1. **V3 wins.**
- V3 vs V6: water's grievance is thin — tastelessness is not a real complaint,
  and the joke is really about the drinker, not the water. **V3 wins clearly.**
- V3 vs V7: an alarm clock is a device, not an everyday essential; the grievance
  is about mornings, not the alarm itself. **V3 wins on criterion 1.**
- V3 vs V8: the snooze button is a feature, not an essential. **V3 wins on criterion 1.**

## Winner: V3

`One star. Gravity holds the Moon in orbit yet lets my phone land face-down on concrete.`

Why it wins:
- Criterion 1: gravity is named in the brief's own examples; the review is
  unmistakably about gravity itself.
- Criterion 2: the phone landing face-down on concrete is a true, daily,
  universally recognised annoyance — gravity doing its job perfectly, just
  never in your favour.
- Criterion 3: deadpan throughout; no winking, no explaining, no exclamation.
- Criterion 4: the sting ("lets my phone land face-down on concrete") is the
  last words of the line.
- Criterion 5: one joke, one contrast (Moon vs phone), 87 bytes — the
  second-shortest variant and the shortest of the serious contenders, which
  also helps the bytes tie-break.

Rejected on purpose: addressing the judge in the text (allowed by the rules,
but it reads as winking and breaks criterion 3); a second joke or extra clause
(criterion 5); any non-ASCII character (costs tie-break bytes).
